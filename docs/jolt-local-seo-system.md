# Jolt Programmatic SEO System

## 1. Strategic SEO Summary

Jolt can win with programmatic SEO because the offer is narrow, local, and repeatable: local service businesses often have the same site problems across cities and industries, but the page still needs to feel specific to the buyer.

The risk is scale without substance. Publishing 1,000 pages at once would be a bad move because Google still evaluates usefulness page by page, and scaled low-value pages can look like doorway pages or scaled content abuse.

The safe path is:

- Start with the highest-intent service, problem, hub, and a few city/industry pages.
- Add only pages that have a distinct user intent and enough unique data to justify themselves.
- Keep draft or noindex status until proof, photos, or local/industry context exists.
- Let Search Console queries and conversion data decide which clusters deserve more pages.

Programmatic SEO should support leads, not just traffic. Every page needs a clear next step, usually the Free Website Teardown, with alternate CTAs for the right page type.

Professional services are part of the system too. For law firms, accountants, financial advisors, consultants, insurance agencies, dental practices, and similar firms, the conversion language should shift away from "get a quote" and toward consultation, intake, booking, or contact.

## 5. Page Scoring and Prioritization Model

Score each page idea 1 to 5:

- Local relevance
- Buyer intent
- Service fit
- Ease of creating unique content
- Conversion potential
- Competition difficulty
- Risk of thin or duplicate content
- Availability of proof or examples

Suggested thresholds:

- Phase 1: 30+ total score, no single critical risk below 3, and proof or context available
- Phase 2: 24 to 29 total score, with a clear reason to exist
- Do not publish yet: below 24, or any page that is mostly duplicated, unsupported, or hard to differentiate

Rule of thumb:

- Attractive keyword, thin page = do not publish yet
- Weak local data but strong service fit = keep as draft or noindex
- Real proof and specific intent beat a bigger keyword every time

## 7. URL Structure

Use clean, layered URLs:

- Geographic hub: `/central-illinois/`
- City: `/locations/bloomington-normal/`
- Industry: `/industries/roofing-companies/`
- City + industry: `/locations/bloomington-normal/roofing-companies/`
- Service: `/services/website-refresh/`
- Problem: `/problems/website-gets-traffic-no-leads/`
- Buying guide: `/guides/local-business-website-refresh-cost/`
- Teardown: `/free-website-teardown/contractors/`
- Case study: `/portfolio/before-after-roofing-refresh/`

Rules:

- Use one canonical URL per intent.
- City pages should link to the hub, relevant industries, and nearby or related cities.
- Industry pages should link to the hub, relevant cities, and problem pages.
- City + industry pages should link to both parent pages and the best-fit service page.
- Buying guides and comparisons should link to services and teardown.
- Include in XML sitemap only if the page is intended to be indexable.
- Use noindex for draft, duplicate, or not-yet-ready pages.

## 8. Internal Linking Strategy

Hub and spoke model:

- The Central Illinois hub links to every main city page, major industry page, and core service page.
- City pages link to the parent hub, one or two core services, a couple of relevant industries, and at least one problem page.
- Industry pages link to the parent hub, a few core cities, relevant problems, and the best-fit service pages.
- City + industry pages link to both parent pages, the relevant service page, the teardown, and one supporting guide.
- Problem pages link to the teardown, relevant service pages, and one comparison or guide page.
- Case studies link back to the matching city, industry, and service page.

Avoid over-linking:

- 4 to 8 useful internal links is enough on most pages.
- Use natural anchor text, not repeated exact-match anchors.

Anchor examples:

- Free Website Teardown
- Core Website Refresh
- website refresh for roofers
- website help for Bloomington-Normal businesses
- consultation page optimization for law firms

Breadcrumbs:

- Home > Central Illinois > Bloomington-Normal
- Home > Industries > Roofing Companies
- Home > Problems > Website Gets Traffic But No Leads

Footer:

- Link only to core commercial pages and the main hub pages.
- Do not bury the footer in a giant list of every city or industry.

## 10. Content Quality Rules

Rules:

- No page may be a city-name swap.
- No unsupported claims, invented reviews, invented case studies, or fake local expertise.
- No keyword stuffing or repetitive AI phrasing.
- Every page must have a useful answer, not just a marketing angle.
- If proof exists, use it. If proof does not exist, say so or stay generic.
- Use plainspoken language and concrete business problems.
- Make FAQs genuinely helpful.
- Make the CTA feel like the natural next step.

Do not publish if:

- It only changes the city name
- It has no unique local, industry, problem, or service insight
- It has no clear search intent
- It has no useful CTA
- It makes unsupported claims
- It invents proof
- It competes with an existing stronger page
- It would feel useless to a real business owner

## 12. Technical SEO Requirements

- Index only pages that are meaningfully unique and intended for search.
- Use noindex for drafts, placeholders, and thin pages.
- Generate XML sitemaps from the publishable page list only.
- Use canonical tags on every page.
- Add `BreadcrumbList` schema everywhere possible.
- Add `LocalBusiness` schema where appropriate and accurate.
- Add `Service` schema to commercial pages.
- Add `FAQPage` schema only when FAQs are visible on the page.
- Add `Organization` schema sitewide if it matches the brand.
- Keep page speed basics tight: compressed images, limited layout shift, no heavy unnecessary scripts.
- Make internal links crawlable in plain HTML.
- Avoid duplicate city pages and duplicate industry pages.
- Track Search Console for impressions, CTR, indexing, and query overlap.

## 19. Final Risk Report

Safest parts:

- Service pages
- Teardown pages
- Problem pages
- Strong hub pages
- Real case studies

Riskiest parts:

- Large city + industry batches
- Buying guides with fake or shaky cost assumptions
- Any page without real proof
- Professional service pages with compliance-sensitive claims

Delay until real proof exists:

- City + industry pages for weaker markets
- Comparison pages that need real differentiation
- Case studies without permission or screenshots

Noindex:

- Draft pages
- Placeholder pages
- Pages waiting on proof
- Thin pages that are useful internally but not ready for Google

Merge:

- Pages that answer the same intent
- Overlapping city pages
- Overlapping comparison pages

Never create:

- Pages that only swap names
- Pages with fake proof
- Pages with no user intent
- Pages made only to inflate count

Signs of low-value scaled content:

- Lots of impressions, weak CTR, weak engagement
- Multiple pages with nearly identical query patterns
- Little or no movement in conversions
- Pages indexed but not retained in results
- Search Console showing overlap instead of distinct intent

Signs the strategy is working:

- Pages rank for long-tail local and answer-style queries
- Visitors click through to the teardown or consultation path
- Search Console shows distinct intent per page type
- More forms and calls from pages with real differentiation
- The site starts to earn traffic from pages that were built for specific buyer problems

Bottom line:

- 1,000 pages is a ceiling, not a launch plan.
- The system should be aggressive, but every page still has to earn its place.
